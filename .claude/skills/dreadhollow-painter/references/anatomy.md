# Anatomy for monsters

Adapted from the `drawing` skill's `figure.md`, `quadruped.md`, `head.md` and
`hands-and-feet.md` (jchapuis/drawing-skill, MIT — see `ATTRIBUTION.md`). Our creatures are
invented, but they are read with the same eyes that read people and animals: a monster whose
joints are wrong reads as a toy, not as a threat.

## A figure fails in its joints

Every check that measures parts passes a figure with no shoulder and no hip. Name the joints
before drawing: neck, shoulders, elbows, wrists, hips, knees, ankles. **More joints is a
better part list; more surface detail is not.**

| Fault | What it looks like | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| The body encloses no space | arm glued to the torso, one dark mass from armpit to hip | leave a wedge of ground between the hanging arm and the body |
| No elbow / no knee | one tube shoulder to hand | two segments overlapping at an angle: the contour **breaks** at the joint |
| Straw legs | constant width, no calf, no ankle | widest at the root, swelling at the calf, narrowing into the ankle; feet with a heel and a sole, never discs |
| Limb wider at the far end | a club instead of an arm | a limb is widest at its root and narrows into the joint it runs into (`limb(w1 > w2)`) |
| Tramline | a line down the middle of a limb reads as two thin limbs | an interior line must die out; name what makes it (a crease, a plate edge) |
| Twinning | both arms, both legs at the same angle | different angles and heights; one arm doing something |
| The box torso | a rectangle with a ball on it | three masses: ribcage, pelvis, head, each tilted; the waist narrows between them |

**The shoulder** is a corner, not a curve: the arm hangs from a point above and outside the
ribcage. For armoured figures the pauldron sits on that corner and overlaps the upper arm.
**The hip** is a side exit: the thigh leaves the side of the pelvis, not its bottom.

## Heads

Two masses and a hinge: a cranium (big, round), a facial wedge hung on its front-lower
quarter, and a jaw slung from a hinge near the ear (that is what lets a maw open). Decide the
**turn** first: our models are in three-quarter view facing right, so the face's centre line
runs near the right edge of the head, the far eye is smaller and closer to it. A brow ridge
shades the eyes; an eye socket is a rounded rhombus, not a circle. A helmet is still a head:
it has a crown, a side plane and a back.

## Hands and feet

A hand or foot fails by becoming **one dark mass where there should be several forms**: a
mitten, a disc, a fist welded to its weapon. A hand holding a weapon is a back sitting on the
haft with fingers curling round and under it, and the haft continues on both sides of the fist.
At game size a hand is a mitt with claws; still, it must read as gripping.

## Beasts and quadrupeds

An animal is **one connected mass**, poll to hoof, before it is any parts. The failure is a
well-made head sitting on a detached slab with floating legs, and the part that breaks is
always the **chest** — the passage from the neck into the ribcage where the forelegs hang.
Block the barrel and chest at the same stage as the head. The ribcage is a deep barrel;
there is no collarbone, so the front end hangs between the shoulder blades and the shoulders
are narrow. Legs: a leg, a gap, a leg — measure the gap. Heads of grazing beasts are shields
(widest at the top), not eggs; horns are cones with a thick base, not wires; ears are leaves
that come to a point.

## Winged, legless and many-limbed things

- **Wings** attach to a shoulder like an arm and fold like one: a leading edge (the "arm"),
  finger struts, membrane between. Folded, they are a mass on the back; spread, they are the
  silhouette break.
- **Serpents, worms, tentacles**: a tube that tapers, overlapping itself in coils; each coil
  is a cylinder with its terminator along its length; the near coil occludes the far one.
- **Amorphous things** (oozes, homunculi, blobs): a sack under gravity — flattened at the
  bottom, bulging low, a highlight near the top-left. Give them one clear focal feature (an
  eye, a core, treasure inside) or they read as "a blob".
- **Constructs and armour**: plates are planes; show where one plate overlaps the next (a lit
  edge over a shadow line), show the gaps at the joints, and never let a torso be one flat
  panel.
